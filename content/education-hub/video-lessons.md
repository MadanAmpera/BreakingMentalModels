  headingAccent: your life.
  lede: Each theory section opens with a video that begins in your personal world — a family argument, a friendship dynamic, a group project, a daily habit — before connecting it to the organisational concept. They are designed to make you say "oh, that's what that is" before you ever read the theory.
lessons:
  - theory: Autocratic · Custodial · Supportive · Collegial
    title: Foundational OB models
    label: Video coming soon
    body: Think about how decisions get made in your family. Does one parent just say "because I said so" — or do you all sit down and talk it through? Does your household run on rules and routines, or on trust and teamwork? This video maps those exact family and friendship dynamics onto the four OB management models — Autocratic, Custodial, Supportive, and Collegial — so you can see which model you've been living inside before you ever set foot in an office.
  - cat: i
    theory: Maslow · Herzberg · McClelland · Alderfer
    title: "Motivation: content theories"
    label: Video coming soon
    body: Why do you push through a late-night study session for something you actually care about, but can't drag yourself to start an assignment you don't? Why does a "well done" from someone you respect feel better than a cash reward? This video walks through Maslow, Herzberg, McClelland and Alderfer using the real motivation patterns from your daily life — revision, friendships, family approval, personal goals — before showing you exactly how the same forces operate in every workplace you'll ever enter.
  - cat: i
    theory: Vroom · Adams · Locke · Skinner
    title: "Motivation: process theories"
    label: Video coming soon
    body: Why do you stop trying at something the moment the reward feels pointless? Why does it sting so much when a sibling or friend gets praised for the same effort you put in and got nothing for? This video uses those exact moments — from family comparisons to personal goal-setting to the feeling of being treated unfairly — to bring Vroom, Adams, Locke and Skinner to life.
  - cat: i
    theory: Big Five · MBTI · Holland
    title: Personality theories
    label: Video coming soon
    body: Are you the one in your friend group who always finishes what they start — or the one who generates all the ideas but loses interest halfway through? This video uses your real patterns with friends, family, and school to explain the Big Five, MBTI and Holland's types — so that when you eventually take a personality assessment at work, you'll understand what it's actually measuring and why it matters for how you work with others.
  - cat: i
    theory: Attribution · Halo effect · Self-serving bias
    title: Perception & attribution
    label: Video coming soon
    body: Have you ever immediately assumed someone was being rude — then found out they were just having a terrible day? That's Attribution Theory, the Halo Effect, and Self-Serving Bias — playing out in your daily life right now. This video unpacks perception and attribution through the snap judgements you make about friends, family, and strangers every single day — so you can spot these biases in yourself before they damage your relationships at work and beyond.
  - cat: i
    theory: Pavlov · Skinner · Bandura · Festinger
    title: Learning theories & attitudes
    label: Video coming soon
    body: Why did you pick up your parents' habits without ever being taught them? Why do you feel uncomfortable when you act against your own values — even when no one else knows? This video makes those invisible learning processes visible — so you understand not just how organisations train people, but how you yourself were shaped long before you ever entered one.
  - cat: i
    theory: Kohlberg · Kant · Bandura
    title: Ethics in Organisational Behaviour
    label: Video coming soon
    body: Have you ever stayed quiet when you saw something unfair happen to a friend — because everyone else stayed quiet too? Ethics isn't something that only happens in boardrooms and corporate scandals. It happens in your group chat, your family dinner, your school corridor, every single day. This video uses those real moral moments to explain Kohlberg's stages, Kant's Categorical Imperative, and Bandura's moral disengagement.
  - cat: g
    theory: Tuckman
    title: Group dynamics & teamwork
    label: Video coming soon
    body: Think about the last group of people you were thrown into — a new class, a sports team, a group project. There was probably an awkward start, maybe a blow-up at some point, then things settled, and eventually the group either clicked or fell apart. That's Tuckman's model — and you've lived through it dozens of times already. When you're placed in a work team for the first time, you'll know exactly which stage you're in — and what to do about it.
  - cat: g
    theory: Trait · Behavioural · Contingency · Transformational
    title: Leadership theories
    label: Video coming soon
    body: Think about the people in your life who have led you — a parent, a coach, a teacher, an older sibling. What made you actually follow them? Leadership theory isn't just about CEOs. It's about every person who has ever influenced your behaviour. This video uses the leaders already in your life to walk through Trait, Behavioural, Contingency and Transformational theories.
  - cat: g
    theory: French & Raven · Shannon-Weaver
    title: Power, politics & communication
    label: Video coming soon
    body: Who has power over you right now — and why do you actually let them? These five power bases aren't just in organisations — they're in your family, your friend group, your school, your social media feed. And the last time a message you sent was completely misunderstood? That's Shannon-Weaver's noise in action. This video decodes power and communication through the relationships you're already navigating every day.
  - cat: g
    theory: Thomas-Kilmann · Fisher & Ury
    title: Conflict & negotiation
    label: Video coming soon
    body: Think about how you handle conflict with a sibling, a close friend, or a parent. Do you go in hard, avoid the conversation entirely, give in to keep the peace — or sit down and actually work it out together? You already have a default conflict style. This video maps the Thomas-Kilmann model and Fisher & Ury's negotiation principles onto your lived experience.
  - cat: o
    theory: Weber · Taylor · Mintzberg
    title: Organisational structure & design
    label: Video coming soon
    body: Think about the difference between your school and a small family business, or between a hospital and a startup. Why does one feel rigid and rule-bound while the other feels chaotic and free? This video uses familiar structures from your everyday life to explain Weber's bureaucracy, Taylor's scientific management, and Mintzberg's configurations — so you'll be able to read any organisation's structure the moment you join it.
  - cat: o
    theory: Schein · Hofstede
    title: Organisational culture
    label: Video coming soon
    body: Every family has a culture. There are unwritten rules about what you talk about at dinner, how you handle disagreements, what's celebrated and what's never mentioned. You didn't choose those rules — you absorbed them. This video uses family and community culture to make Schein's three levels and Hofstede's dimensions feel like something you've been an expert in your whole life.
  - cat: o
    theory: Lewin · Kotter · ADKAR
    title: Management of change
    label: Video coming soon
    body: Think about a time something in your life changed and you resisted it — moving schools, a family restructure, a new routine being forced on you. Lewin's Unfreeze-Change-Refreeze, Kotter's 8 steps, and the ADKAR model describe the exact psychological journey every human goes through when their world shifts — change not as something that happens to organisations, but as something that happens to people.
  - cat: o
    theory: Open systems · General systems
    title: Systems theory
    label: Video coming soon
    body: Have you ever noticed that when one person in your family is stressed, everyone else feels it? Or that fixing one problem in a group project somehow creates a new one somewhere else? That's systems thinking. This video explains Open Systems Theory and General Systems principles — so you'll understand why you can never change just one thing, and why the whole is always more than the sum of its parts.
  - cat: o
    theory: Karasek · JD-R
    title: Work stress & well-being
    label: Video coming soon
    body: Think about the difference between a subject you love — where the workload feels energising — and one you dread, where even a small task feels crushing. The pressure isn't always the problem. What's missing is often the control, the support, or the sense that it matters. That's Karasek's Demand-Control model and the JD-R framework — playing out in your study life, your home life, and your social life right now.
