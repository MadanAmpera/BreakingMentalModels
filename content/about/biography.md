I am Dr. Ankit Agarwal, and my journey into education began at the tender age of five. Growing up in India, I was captivated by the titles held by my relatives — Doctors, Engineers, Advocates — and I found myself fascinated by the respect and admiration these titles commanded. At that young age, I dreamed of earning a title of my own, believing that education was the key to unlocking my future. However, like many children, I found traditional schooling a bit tedious and much preferred playing cricket. In the 1990s, I, like countless other children, idolised cricket legends such as Sachin Tendulkar and Don Bradman. Their humble demeanour and relentless passion for the game inspired me.

I wanted to emulate their success on the field, and while I considered myself above average, I never quite reached the heights I aspired to. This dream was further complicated by the passing of my grandparents, who were incredibly close to me. Their loss was profound and prompted me to leave India, feeling as though I was walking away from my dreams and the cricketing opportunities that once seemed within reach.

My journey took me to Dublin, Ireland, where I pursued a Diploma in Business Studies and later a Bachelor's in Management Information Systems and Management Accounting. Upon arriving in Ireland, I faced significant struggles, juggling multiple jobs to support myself. I transitioned from overnight security work to university classes, then to afternoon shifts at a petrol station, and even delivered food for local restaurants — all while maintaining a 97% attendance record at university to keep my visa valid. The hustle was relentless, and I often changed clothes in the car between jobs, but I was determined to succeed.

After my time in Ireland, I moved to the UK to pursue a Master's in Computer Systems and Software Engineering. I landed my first full-time job at PC World in Maidstone, Kent, and eventually moved to the US on an H1B visa. Unfortunately, I was duped by a company there, which led me to sue them. I fought the case while providing depositions via Skype from India, ultimately winning and seeing the company closed down. While I had to give 80% of my winnings to my wonderful lawyers who fought for me, it was a significant victory that showcased my resilience.

In pursuit of a new direction, I applied for permanent residency in Australia, successfully securing it within six to nine months. This pivotal moment led me to reach out to Dr. Peter Sandiford at the University of Adelaide, hoping he would supervise my PhD thesis. After waiting over a year for a response, I decided to take matters into my own hands. I flew to Australia for a day, determined to meet him in person and discuss my aspirations. Although I ultimately realised I needed to complete another Master's degree, this experience ignited a passion for academia that I hadn't fully recognised before.

Upon arriving in Adelaide, I lived in a hostel called Blue Galah on King William Street, where I had little money as I searched for jobs. To cover my rent, I took on tasks like making beds and cleaning toilets, receiving just a bowl of rice in the evenings. Despite the hardships, I found joy in the camaraderie of fellow backpackers, who appreciated my cooking and shared in the experience of learning together. I applied for over 350 jobs, securing only four interviews and facing numerous rejections. One setback included failing my driving test, which prevented me from securing a job I had hoped for. However, Matthew Giles offered me an alternative job in door-knocking, which marked the beginning of my career in Adelaide.

Throughout these struggles, I learned that each challenge was a small success step, shaping my resilience and determination. In hindsight, these experiences, while difficult, paved the way for my eventual achievements. In 2021, I secured my first lecturing position at the University of Adelaide, marking the beginning of a new chapter in my career.

::pull
My story is one of resilience, curiosity, and the belief that every challenge presents an opportunity for growth. Education is not just about acquiring knowledge; it's about connecting with others, sharing experiences, and growing together.
::
