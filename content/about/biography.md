I am Dr. Ankit Agarwal, and my journey into education began at the tender age of five. Growing up in India, I was captivated by the titles held by my relatives — Doctors, Engineers, Lawyers — and I found myself fascinated by the respect and admiration these titles commanded. At that young age, I dreamed of earning a title of my own, believing that education was the key to unlocking my future. However, like many children, I found traditional schooling a bit tedious and much preferred playing cricket. In the 1990s, I, like countless other children, idolised cricket legends such as Sachin Tendulkar, the late Shane Warne and Glenn McGrath. Their humble demeanour and relentless passion for the game inspired me.

I wanted to emulate their success on the field, and while I considered myself above average, I never quite reached the heights I aspired to. This dream was further complicated by the passing of my grandparents, who were incredibly close to me. Their loss was profound and prompted me to leave India, feeling as though I was walking away from my dreams and the cricketing opportunities that once seemed within reach.

My journey took me to Dublin, Republic of Ireland, where I pursued a Diploma in Business Studies and later a Bachelor's in Management Information Systems and Management Accounting. Upon arriving in Dublin, I faced significant struggles (in hindsight, learning opportunities), juggling multiple jobs to support myself. I transitioned from overnight security work to classes at the Dublin Business School, then to afternoon shifts at a petrol station, and even delivered food for local restaurants — all while maintaining a full attendance record at the Dublin Business School to keep my visa valid. The hustle was relentless, and I often changed clothes on the bus between jobs, but I was determined to succeed.

Since I arrived in Dublin, I started playing cricket for North Kildare Cricket Club in the hope that I could continue what I had left back at home. I did well for three years, and then my visa didn't renew and I left the dream behind once again, this time leading me to the UK.

After my time in Dublin, I moved to the UK to pursue a Master's in Computer Systems and Software Engineering at the University of Greenwich, London. I landed my first full-time job at PC World in Maidstone, Kent, which I did passionately and well for a year until finishing my Master's, then moving to South East London to continue it in the Charlton branch on a work permit until the visa ran out in 2008 and I had to return to India. After learning various computer languages in India, I eventually moved to the US on an H1B visa. Unfortunately, I was duped by a company there, which led me to sue them. I fought the case while providing depositions via Skype from India, ultimately winning and seeing the company closed down. While I had to give 80% of my winnings to my wonderful lawyers who fought for me in California, it was a significant victory that made me realise, "one must never give in to the unethical conduct of people or organisations." If I give in, countless others in my shoes would continue suffering; at least that was my learning from this whole experience of naivety.

In pursuit of a new direction, I gained experiences in India in different companies in different cities in different roles, from sales to business development to IT, to healthcare to publishing to clinical trials and paperwork to starting on my entrepreneurial journey. During this period, I also applied for permanent residency in Australia, successfully securing it within seven months of the application. This pivotal moment led me to reach out to Dr. Peter Sandiford at the University of Adelaide, hoping he would supervise my PhD thesis. I flew to Australia for a day, determined to meet him in person and discuss my aspirations. Although I ultimately realised I needed to complete another Master's degree, this experience ignited a passion for academia that I hadn't fully recognised before.

Upon arriving in Adelaide, I lived in a hostel called Blue Galah on King William Street (now demolished), where I had little money as I searched for jobs. To cover my rent, I took on tasks like making beds and cleaning toilets, receiving a bowl of rice in the evenings. Despite what I then identified as hardships, I found joy in the camaraderie of fellow backpackers, who appreciated my cooking and shared in the experience of learning together. I applied for over 350 jobs, securing only four interviews and facing numerous rejections. One setback included failing my driving test, which prevented me from securing a job I had hoped for. However, Matthew Giles from the Modern Home Improvement Group offered me an alternative job in door-knocking, talking about home improvement products with South Australian residents, which marked the beginning of my career in Adelaide.

Throughout these struggles, I learned that each challenge was a small success step, shaping my resilience and determination. In hindsight, these experiences, while difficult, paved the way for my eventual achievements. In 2021, after close to six years of sessional lecturing, I secured my first full-time lecturing position at the University of Adelaide, marking the beginning of a new chapter in my career.

::pull
My story is one of those seeming struggles and a hindsight learning that led me to discover resilience, curiosity, and the belief that every challenge I was introduced to also came with an opportunity for growth. Education is not just about acquiring knowledge; it's about connecting with others, dialoguing on experiences, and giving each other an opportunity to grow co-constructively.
::
